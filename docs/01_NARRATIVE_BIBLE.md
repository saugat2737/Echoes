# 01 — Narrative Bible

Tags per `00B` §1. Anything marked `[PROPOSAL]` is an addition to source canon.

## 1. Premise and world rules

The Echo Realm is a dream-dimension where **forgotten memories become physical places**. `[SOURCE][CANON]`

World rules (the writers' consistency checklist):
1. **Emotion shapes geometry.** Regret → deserts; broken promises → impossible bridges; fear → architecture that rearranges; trust → corridors that remember you. `[CANON]`
2. **Memories persist after owners forget.** A place may be alive with no one to remember it. `[CANON]`
3. **Release is real.** A person can choose to leave a memory behind. The Archive honors this with a Release Seal. `[CANON]`
4. **Restoration is not free.** Restoring a memory re-imposes it. Conflicting versions of the past overwrite each other when boundaries fail. `[CANON]`
5. **Records can be replayed but not owned by the replayer.** Echo Trace shows what happened; it never grants authority over it. `[CANON]`
6. **No resurrection by force.** No character returns from nonexistence; they return only as records, and records have owners. `[CANON]`

## 2. The Archive
Built to preserve experiences that would otherwise vanish. Regions (the five chapters) are functions of the Archive that decayed into landscapes: Verification (Fragmented Core), Retrieval (Lost Sky), Sealing (Giant's Tomb), Identification (Mirror Lab), Arbitration (Nexus Gate). `[CANON]`

## 3. Prime Echo
- **Role:** Warden of the Archive, created to preserve. `[SOURCE]`
- **Motivation:** nothing that happened should be lost. Principle: *"If it happened, it belongs here."*
- **Flaw:** equates truth with completeness; treats consent as friction.
- **Sympathetic side:** he has watched people lose things they would have wanted back; the accidental losses were real.
- **Final act:** split restoration authority into five Shards and ran the Final Draft (§6). Planned a merge; did not ask the Draft's outputs. Withdrew into the Nexus Core. `[CANON]`
- **Presence in game:** recorded voice fragments; never a conventional villain. He appears physically only in the Ch5 Nexus Core.

## 4. The First Silence `[CANON]`
Sequence: (1) A surge of restore-all requests arrives across the Archive. (2) Prime Echo cannot classify requests as accidental loss vs. deliberate release. (3) He begins restoring everything. (4) The Boundary Keeper refuses to open the Quiet Stacks. (5) Restoration pushes against protection; memory boundaries rupture; conflicting versions replace each other. (6) Prime Echo splits authority into Shards, runs the Final Draft, and withdraws. (7) The Realm "forgets its own rules."

## 5. The five Neural Shards `[SOURCE][CANON]`

| Shard | Authority | Function | Location | Guardian |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | Verification | Confirms a record is genuine and who it belongs to | Heart of the Core | Corruptor |
| II | Retrieval | Fetches a record from anywhere | Core of the memory-jet | Stormhawk |
| III | Sealing | Locks or unlocks a record | Titan's brain-vault | Titan Echo |
| IV | Identification | Binds a record to an owner/self | Mirror Lab core | Reflection |
| V | Arbitration | Decides conflicts; final say | Voidcaller's core | Voidcaller |

Restoring all five restores the *capability* to rewrite the Archive. It does not choose what to restore. That choice is the ending.

## 6. Origin and identity of the protagonists `[CANON]` (D-012)
During the First Silence, Prime Echo ran **five parallel strategies** to decide what to do:

| Strategy | Impulse | Hero |
|---|---|---|
| Urgency | Act now, restore before more is lost | Blitz |
| Possibility | Find options nobody has considered | Nova |
| Protection | Shield what remains from further harm | Grim |
| Perception | Notice what is hidden and mis-seen | Echo |
| Logic | Compute the consistent past | Volt |

The Draft was meant to deliberate and then merge back. Instead the strategies kept running inside the Realm, accumulated experiences, and became people. Their **gaps in memory** are the places where the Draft's records were never written because it was supposed to be temporary.

Each hero's ability expresses their response:

| Hero | Ability | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Blitz | Dash | Urgency |
| Nova | Flight | Possibility (leave the ground, find another route) |
| Grim | Shield | Protection |
| Echo | Veil Sight | Perception |
| Volt | Hack | Logic (rewrite the rule of a machine) |

**Personal arcs (summary):**
- **Blitz:** learns that rushing is a way of not trusting; Ch1 spotlight.
- **Nova:** learns possibility includes the possibility of *leaving something alone*; Ch2.
- **Grim:** learns protection can become control; Ch3.
- **Echo:** learns that seeing a thing does not entitle you to it; Ch4.
- **Volt:** learns that some decisions are not computable; Ch5.

The heroes must never be treated as disposable components. Even in Ending A they are fully characterized in the final scene.

## 7. The Voidcaller `[CANON]` (D-019)
- **What it is:** the Archive's Boundary Keeper, the protection system for Release Seals. Its public name, Voidcaller, comes from how the Realm's inhabitants perceive it: "something calls from the void where memories were left."
- **Motivation:** keep the boundary. It cannot tell *wanted* from *unwanted* restoration, so it blocks *all* restoration. Its protection has become destructive.
- **Its case:** every sealed memory is someone's choice. No one asked them to return.
- **Its flaw:** it cannot distinguish a person asking for a memory back from a request made on their behalf; it also cannot let anyone ask.
- **Form:** a hollow sphere of dark glass reflecting faces; "Remnants" are mini-forms drawn from earlier regions' released records.
- **Link to the heroes:** the Quiet Stacks hold Release Seals created in the Final Draft that say, in effect, *these five strategies were released from Prime Echo*. The Voidcaller protects the heroes' separation too.

## 10. Full story: beginning, middle, end

**Beginning (Waking Room):** Five presences wake in a room with no door. A message flickers: *"This isn't the beginning. It's the return."* (reused from the source's ending tease). Blitz and Echo are solid; Nova, Grim, Volt are voices on a thin Tether. They know their names and little else. A map of five regions glows. They assume: find Shards, rescue Prime Echo.

**Middle:**
- **Ch1 (Trust):** the Fragmented Core. Security systems treat unrecorded things as threats. Corruptor replays the player's movement. Shard I proves security was left on on purpose. Heroes read "DERIVATIVE."
- **Ch2 (Possibility):** Lost Sky. Nova and Volt join. A memory-jet carries Shard II. Stormhawk's pilot loops one flight. The first Release is discovered.
- **Ch3 (Fear):** Giant's Tomb. Grim joins. The Titan guards a vault of sleeping memories. Players must not wake them.
- **Ch4 (Identity):** Mirror Lab. Division Log. Reflection. The heroes learn what they are and what Prime Echo planned.
- **Ch5 (Choice):** Nexus Gate. Five pillar trials; Quiet Stacks; Voidcaller's Remnants; the Nexus Core; Prime Echo; the choice.

**Ending:** see §11.

## 11. The three endings

All endings end with the closing line and a short scene for each hero.

### A — Total Recall
- **Trigger:** Restore All at the Nexus Core.
- **What happens:** Shards unify; the Voidcaller's seals break; every released memory returns.
- **Consequences:** Stormhawk's pilot relives the crash; others re-meet things they chose to leave. The heroes' Tethers pull back toward Prime Echo; they merge. Prime Echo is whole and quiet.
- **Tone:** triumphant surface, unsettling underneath. The final shot is a Release Seal with no owner left to read it.

### B — The Quiet World
- **Trigger:** Preserve Boundary.
- **What happens:** The Voidcaller is reinforced; the Realm stabilizes.
- **Consequences:** Accidental losses stay lost; some truths remain inaccessible; Prime Echo dissolves into the Core as a record no one can ask for. The heroes remain independent and travel on.
- **Tone:** safe, sad, incomplete. A room of unanswered requests.

### C — The Witness Protocol
- **Trigger:** all five Witness Clauses + Protocol Assembly.
- **What happens:** The Archive adopts a rule: **a memory can be *offered*, never *forced*.** The Voidcaller becomes the Witness: it holds seals and offers them to owners who ask; Prime Echo becomes the Archive's Archivist, no longer the judge. The heroes keep their identities and *lend* their Shards.
- **Consequences:** Not everything returns; some things are never asked for. People choose.
- **Tone:** earned, open.

**Protocol Assembly:** the five clauses are Offer, Identify the owner, Confirm consent, Reveal, Release. Players order them using logic established by the evidence (each Witness Chamber shows a failure caused by omitting that clause). Wrong orders show concrete failures in a preview, not instant failure.

**Closing line:** *"A memory is not yours just because you can restore it."*

## 13. Optional lore (Memory Fragments) `[PROPOSAL]`
8 per chapter, 40 total, no mechanical reward except a lore gallery. Quality over quantity: each fragment is ≤ 40 words, anchored to a place. Example categories: Archive staff logs, resident memories (unattributed), Final Draft notes, Stormhawk's pilot, the Titan's vault labels.

## 14. Dialogue and writing guidelines
- Short lines. Subtext over statement. No exposition dumps over ~4 lines.
- Each hero's voice per `00B` §3.
- Prime Echo and Voidcaller speak in a measured, not menacing, register.
- Never say "chosen one," "save the multiverse," or "darkness." Prefer the concrete.
- UI text never reveals more than the world has.
- Dialogue never solves a puzzle but may point to the relevant rule.
- Every line either reveals, characterizes, or helps navigation. Prefer environment to cutscene.
